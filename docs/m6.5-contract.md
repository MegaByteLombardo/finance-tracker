# M6.5 contract — backend ↔ presentation

**Status:** frozen · **Milestone:** M6.5 — Weekly & Monthly Recap

This document extends [m6-contract.md](m6-contract.md), which **remains in force
in full**. Nothing in M6 is superseded: the recap reads the same snapshot, the
same flows and the same rates, by the same rules. It adds calendar periods, a
comparison of every category, the largest expenses, the movement of each account
and four fixed indicators.

M6.5 adds no table, no column, no index and no migration. Schema version stays 13.

## The one rule, unchanged

The presentation layer never writes SQL, never opens a transaction, never imports
a repository, and never performs financial arithmetic. Every figure on the recap
is computed by `internal/analytics` and arrives formatted, and so does every
direction and every verdict. A template chooses a CSS class from a boolean the
backend decided; it never compares two amounts to decide one itself.

---

## 1. The question

> How did this week, or this month, go compared with the one before it? What
> went up, what went down, and where do I stand now against where I was?

## 2. Periods (TD-072)

| Granularity | A period | The previous one |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `WEEK` | Monday to Sunday | The week before |
| `MONTH` | The 1st to the last day | The month before, of its own length |

- **A period that has ended is compared with the whole previous one**, even when
  the two differ in length (31 days against 30, or against February). Those are
  the months a person means. The daily average is shown beside every total so
  the comparison stays fair.
- **A period in progress is cut at today and compared like for like**: the
  previous period is cut at the same number of elapsed days, never beyond its own
  end.

  | Today | Current | Previous |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | 2026-10-12 | 2026-10-01 → 2026-10-12 | 2026-09-01 → 2026-09-12 |
  | 2026-03-31 | 2026-03-01 → 2026-03-31 | 2026-02-01 → 2026-02-28 |
  | Wed 2026-10-14 | 2026-10-12 → 2026-10-14 | 2026-10-05 → 2026-10-07 |

  Twelve days against thirty would read as a fall in spending that did not
  happen.
- **Default:** the last period that has fully ended. On 2026-10-12 the monthly
  recap is September against August and the weekly one is 5–11 October against
  28 September – 4 October.
- **Range:** from the period of the first movement in the ledger to the period
  in progress. An anchor outside it is clamped, never an error. A previous period
  that ends before the first movement does not exist: `HasPrevious = false`, and
  every comparison is `NO_PREVIOUS_PERIOD`.
- "Today" is `database.TodayAt(now)`, the Venezuelan calendar, from the injected
  clock (TD-051).

## 3. Figures

| Figure | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Opening, closing | `NetWorthAt(from − 1)` and `NetWorthAt(to)`. Archived accounts are valued (TD-064). |
| Change | `compare(opening, closing)`, unavailable if either end is incomplete (TD-067). `PreviousChange` is the same over the previous period. |
| Cash flow | `buildCashFlow` for both periods from **one** `ListFlows(previous.from, to)`. M6's rules: `INCOME` and `EXPENSE` only, each at its own day's rate, rounded once per category (TD-068). |
| Income, expense and net cash flow changes | `compare` of the two totals. Unavailable when a flow of the kind compared had no rate on its day. |
| Savings rate change | The difference of the two rates in basis points ("percentage points"). Defined only when both rates are. |
| Daily average expense | The total over the period's days, rounded once, half to even, for each period; then `compare`. |
| Categories | Every category of either period, matched by id, **none folded into "other"**: the one that moved most may be small. Each has current, previous, `Change`, `Direction` and `Share` of the current total. Sorted by the size of the movement, largest first. |
| Largest expenses | Up to 5 individual expenses, each valued at its own day's rate and rounded once, ranked in the base currency; ties by date, then id, both descending (TD-074). |
| Accounts | Each account's balance at both ends in its own currency, the exact difference, and the difference of its two valued lines in the base currency. An account that is on one side only held nothing on the other: zero, needing no rate. |

### Directions

| `Direction` | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `UP`, `DOWN`, `SAME` | Both periods have the category; the current figure is higher, lower or equal. |
| `NEW` | Nothing in the previous period. |
| `GONE` | Nothing in this period. |
| `UNKNOWN` | No previous period, or a flow of that kind had no rate on its day. |

The screen shows `UP` and `NEW` as "subieron", `DOWN` and `GONE` as "bajaron",
and `SAME` in neither.

### Indicators (TD-073)

Four fixed comparisons, each with a verdict of `IMPROVED`, `WORSENED`,
`UNCHANGED` or `UNAVAILABLE`:

| Key | Improved when | Read from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `NET_WORTH` | the net worth grew over the period | `Change` |
| `NET_CASH_FLOW` | it is higher than the previous period's | `NetCashFlowChange` |
| `EXPENSES` | the daily average expense is lower than the previous period's | `ExpenseDailyAverageChange` |
| `SAVINGS_RATE` | it is higher than the previous period's | `SavingsRateChange` |

An undefined comparison is `UNAVAILABLE`, never a guess. `Summary` counts the
verdicts so the header can say "3 de 4 indicadores mejoraron". Nothing weighs one
indicator against another, and nothing recommends anything: the screen states
each rule beside its verdict.

### Missing rates

Exactly M6's rules. A missing rate at either end makes the net worth's change
unavailable, and the screen lists each currency, its balance, the day and a link
to the prefilled manual-rate form. An expense with no rate is left out of the
totals and out of the ranking, and `UnvaluedExpenseCount` says how many there
were. No amount is estimated.

## 4. Analytics (`internal/analytics`)

```go
type Granularity string // "WEEK" | "MONTH"
func ParseGranularity(raw string) (Granularity, bool)
func WeekContaining(day time.Time) Period
func MonthContaining(day time.Time) Period
func CalendarPeriod(g Granularity, day time.Time) Period
func StepCalendarPeriod(g Granularity, day time.Time, n int) Period
func LastClosed(g Granularity, today time.Time) Period
func RecapPeriodsFor(g Granularity, day, today time.Time) RecapPeriods

type RecapRequest struct { Granularity Granularity; Anchor time.Time; HasAnchor bool }
func (s *Service) Recap(ctx context.Context, req RecapRequest) (Recap, error)
```

## 5. Ledger (`internal/ledger`)

```go
func (s *Service) ListExpenses(ctx context.Context, from, to time.Time) ([]ExpenseItem, error)
```

The query is one row per `EXPENSE` transaction in the range, both ends included,
in its own currency, with `AmountMinor` positive. The WHERE clause names the type
it accepts (TD-045). A range that ends before it starts is `ErrInvalidInput`.

## 6. Cost

**Seven queries whatever the period**: the snapshot's five, `ListFlows` for both
periods together, and `ListExpenses` for the current one. There is no query per
day, per account or per category (TD-065).

## 7. The presentation surface (`internal/web`)

`AnalyticsService` gains `Recap(ctx, RecapRequest) (Recap, error)`. An unknown
granularity reaching the adapter is `ErrInvalidInput`. `ParseRecapRequest` reads
`?period=week|month` (default `month`) and `?start=YYYY-MM-DD`; anything
malformed is ignored.

| Route | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /recap?period=&start=` | The desktop recap. `NavRecap` in the main navigation. |
| `GET /mobile/recap?period=&start=` | The same recap, mobile layout, reached from Inicio. The bottom bar keeps its four tabs. |

The granularity selector and the ← → arrows are plain links that htmx enhances
with `hx-select` (`#recap`, `#mobile-recap`). Switching granularity keeps the
period's start. A failure to compute degrades to a message on the page, never a
500. The dashboard's cash-flow card and the mobile Inicio link to the recap.

### Desktop

Selector and arrows; the period's title, with "en curso · parcial" when in
progress; the closing net worth with its change and the opening figure; the
indicators with their rules; warnings for missing rates and unvalued expenses;
the cash flow against the previous period beside the change by account;
categories that rose, categories that fell, and income by category; the largest
expenses at full width, each linking to its edit form.

### Mobile

The same figures, stacked.

## 8. Not in M6.5

Budgets, goals, alerts, notifications and recommendations; any advice text; a
weighted or combined score; a decomposition of the net worth's change into cash
flow and exchange-rate effect; realized or unrealized FX gains; custom date
ranges; quarterly or yearly recaps; a stored or emailed recap; snapshot tables,
caches, background jobs and a JSON API.
